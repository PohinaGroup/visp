## 1. Sign in

Open [visp-stream.com](https://visp-stream.com), press **Try VISP free**, then sign in with Twitch, Kick, or Google. VISP uses that login
only to identify your account.

## 2. Authorize Direct

The setup wizard asks three things:

1. **Where do you go live?** Choose Twitch, Kick, or YouTube. **Somewhere else**
   skips Direct and sends the feed to OBS instead.
2. **How will you send video?** The VISP app is recommended. The browser
   publisher and third-party encoders are under **Use something else**.
3. **Authorize streaming** so VISP can resolve the destination when output
   starts. Twitch and Kick keys are never shown to the publisher or stored as
   separate values. YouTube Direct creates a new public broadcast and uses its
   ingest destination.

Already know your way around? **Skip setup, go to dashboard** on the first
screen skips the wizard. You can switch between Direct and OBS later from the
**Mode: Direct · change** menu at the top of the dashboard.

## 3. Publish from a phone or browser

The last setup screen creates your stream links. Install the native app or open
the browser publisher, sign in with the same account, allow camera and
microphone access, and press **Go Live**. VISP checks
provider authorization and relay encoder capacity before capture starts.

## 4. Verify Direct

The same screen runs **Test your connection**. Wait until the platform output
reports **Platform output is live**. A contribution feed connected to the relay
is not enough. You may skip this test, but the setup remains marked unverified.
Choose **Finish** to open the dashboard.

## 5. Optionally add OBS

OBS is not needed for Direct. If you want it for monitoring, recording, or
scenes, open the dashboard **Destinations** tab and use **OBS read
credentials**. Install and pair the OBS Studio 31 plugin for one-click Media
Sources, or copy the SRT read URL into an OBS Media Source manually. OBS
receives the contribution feed, not the distribution encode delivered to
viewers.

1. Download the package for your OS from the project's
   [GitHub Releases](https://github.com/PohinaGroup/visp/releases), install it,
   and restart OBS.
2. In OBS, open **Tools → VISP Remote Control** and click **Sign in with
   browser**.
3. Sign in to VISP, approve the displayed code, and return to OBS.
4. Under **Publishing devices**, choose **Add to scene** for the device you
   want.

## Publisher details

### Phone app

Install the iOS (App Store) or Android (Google Play) build from the
[download page](https://visp-stream.com/download). Sign in with the same Twitch,
Kick, or Google account. The app creates a publishing device for that phone
automatically.

Pick the camera and microphone in settings, confirm **READY**, then tap
**Go Live**.

### Browser app

Prefer a quick test without installing? Open
[stream.visp-stream.com](https://stream.visp-stream.com) in current Chrome,
Edge, or Safari, sign in, and publish over WebRTC.

### Confirm the optional OBS picture

With the phone or browser live, select the scene that holds the device's Media
Source in OBS. The
Media Source should show your camera feed. If the preview is black, wait a few
seconds for SRT/WebRTC to connect, then check that the matching device shows
**Live** on the dashboard **Devices** tab.

The OBS chat overlay Browser Source stays blank until a chat message arrives;
append `&debug=1` to its URL to show connection status.

### Control optional OBS from your phone

When the plugin is paired, the phone app can switch scenes and start or stop
the home OBS stream. Open the OBS control UI, pick a scene (for example
**Scene** or **Fallback**), and confirm the change in OBS within about two
seconds.

You can also open
[remote.visp-stream.com](https://remote.visp-stream.com) in a browser for the
dedicated OBS Remote control surface.

With Direct, you can optionally turn on a BRB card on the dashboard **BRB**
tab (off by default) so the relay holds the broadcast on it while the phone
reconnects. With OBS, keep OBS on a fallback scene instead — see
[Encoders and fallback](/docs/broadcaster-setup) for Advanced Scene Switcher
macros.
