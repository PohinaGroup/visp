VISP Direct forwards one publishing device to any combination of Twitch, Kick,
and YouTube without OBS in the path. It is the default platform output. Before publishing, VISP
reserves encoder capacity on the assigned relay; a full relay blocks Go Live
instead of silently starting without a platform output.

## What changes

Without Direct, your device does the full encode and the relay forwards that
bitstream to OBS untouched. With Direct enabled on a device:

- your device still encodes, because SRT needs a compressed feed to upload;
- the relay runs a **separate distribution encode per destination** — H.264
  video and stereo AAC audio, always re-encoded, never stream-copied;
- each destination is its own process, so one platform failing does not
  interrupt the other.

VISP Native uses a lower Direct contribution bitrate while keeping 1080p
available; platform quality comes from the relay encode.

Each selected platform consumes a relay forwarding slot and a separate encode.
Selecting several platforms increases relay CPU and outbound bandwidth, but it
does not add another upload from your phone.

## BRB hold

When **Never drop again** is enabled, a lost ingest keeps each Direct output
open. Accounts in the BRB highlights beta can upload up to five short H.264 MP4
clips in the web dashboard. Enabled clips play in order and loop; the current
still BRB card remains the fallback when the flag is off, no clips are enabled,
or the relay cannot play them. The playlist and its audio/message preferences
are captured when BRB starts, so edits apply on the next BRB.

Returning to live or choosing **End broadcast** interrupts the hold. Highlight
configuration is web-only; the native BRB controls are unchanged.

## Choose where the program is made

- **Cloud Studio** burns the last saved web Studio scene into Direct. It supports
  up to three scenes, eight layers per scene, two public HTTPS browser sources,
  and one VISP alert layer. Save is explicit: unsaved edits never reach a live
  program. Native publishes with the last saved program and opens the web editor.
- **I use OBS** keeps the existing workflow. VISP does not host OBS; your own OBS
  owns scenes, overlays, plugins, recording, and the platform output.

The program preview is normally under three seconds behind ingest. A saved live
edit is normally applied in under two seconds. These are operating targets, not
hard customer SLAs. If Studio is unhealthy or killed, Direct falls back to
camera-only passthrough and retries; saved scenes remain intact.

## Enable it

1. During setup, choose Twitch, Kick, or YouTube and authorize streaming. Existing users
   can instead open **Direct output** on the dashboard.
2. Next to a destination, choose **Authorize streaming**. This re-opens the
	provider's consent screen and asks for the permission Direct needs.
   VISP requests every permission you had already granted at the same time, so
   authorizing Direct never switches off chat or title and category editing.
3. Enable any combination of **Twitch**, **Kick**, and **YouTube** for the device.

Outputs are saved as the account default. The newest device takes ownership at
Go Live when the previous owner is offline. A running device is never
interrupted automatically.

## Landscape and portrait together

Accounts with portrait Direct output enabled can add a separate **Portrait**
destination from the dashboard or phone settings. Choose **Add portrait
output**, position the 9:16 crop over the landscape contribution, and save.
The landscape destination remains a passthrough distribution encode while the
portrait destination applies the saved crop and scales it to 1080×1920.

Portrait uses its own forwarder slot. You can save framing while the relay is
full, but the portrait destination will not start until a slot is free;
landscape may still go live. Each side reports status independently. See the
[portrait Direct output guide](/docs/portrait-direct-output) for the full flow.

## Your stream key

You are never asked to paste a stream key. At stream start VISP fetches a
Twitch or Kick key with the consent you granted, builds the destination URL in
memory, hands it to the forwarding process, and drops it when the stream ends.
For YouTube, VISP creates a public broadcast, binds a stream, and uses the
returned RTMPS ingest destination. Provider credentials are never sent to the
web or phone app or stored as separate database values.

The relay-to-OBS workflow is unchanged and still never touches a stream key.

## Using OBS at the same time

OBS can keep reading the feed for monitoring or recording. Do not point OBS at
a platform that Direct already owns — that is two publishers on one stream key,
and the platform will reject or interrupt one of them.

What OBS reads is your device's contribution feed, not the encode the platform
receives. To check what viewers actually see, watch the platform.

## Platform rules

- **Twitch.** Its simulcasting terms prohibit showing another platform's
  activity on the Twitch stream, so do not burn Kick chat into the video.
  Floating chat in the phone app stays fine — only you see it.
- **Kick.** Partners must switch on Kick's own Multistreaming toggle before
  streaming to both. Kick currently reduces Partner Program payout for the
  duration of a multistreaming session. VISP does not toggle this for you.
- **YouTube.** Direct creates a new public broadcast when publishing starts and
  uses automatic start and stop. Set the default title before going live and
  verify the public watch page from a muted device.

Title and category editing is unchanged; Direct adds no metadata behavior.

## When something fails

Each destination shows its own state and last error under the device.

- **A provider is unavailable.** VISP retries that destination with backoff and
  leaves the other one running.
- **Permission was revoked at the platform.** The destination fails and the
  card offers **Reauthorize**.
- **"Direct is at capacity."** The relay is at its concurrent-forwarder limit.
  Your relay-to-OBS path keeps working; try the Direct output again shortly.
- **VISP is unreachable when your device connects.** No Direct output starts.
  A stream already running keeps forwarding until the source disconnects.
