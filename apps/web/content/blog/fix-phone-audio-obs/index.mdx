When a remote phone camera looks right in OBS but sounds wrong, do not start by
changing every audio setting. First identify whether the problem is **silence,
a duplicate signal, a fixed timing difference, or a level problem**. Each has a
different fix.

In a VISP workflow, the phone sends one camera-and-microphone contribution feed
to the relay, and OBS receives that feed as a Media Source. OBS still owns the
final Twitch or Kick mix. That separation gives you useful checkpoints: the
phone meter, the VISP device state, the OBS mixer, a local recording, and the
public platform output.

![The phone captures one microphone, VISP relays the combined audio and video feed, and OBS is where silence, duplicate paths, timing, and final levels are checked](./phone-audio-checkpoints.svg)

## Start with the symptom, not a guessed fix

Use the shortest test that separates one fault from another:

| What you observe | Check first |
| --- | --- |
| No movement on the phone microphone meter | Phone permission and selected microphone |
| Phone meter moves, but the VISP device is not Live | Phone-to-relay connection |
| Device is Live, but no meter moves in OBS | Correct Media Source and current read URL |
| OBS meter moves, but recording or stream is silent | Mute state, monitoring mode, and active output track |
| One voice is heard twice | A duplicated capture or monitoring path |
| Audio is always early by the same amount | Delay the earlier source in OBS |
| Sync changes during the session | Reconnect and investigate load or an unstable source before adding a fixed offset |
| Voice distorts on loud words | Reduce gain at the earliest stage that is clipping |

Make a 20-second local recording after each change. Listening only through
live monitoring can mislead you because the monitoring path has its own delay
and routing.

## Understand the phone-to-OBS audio path

The native VISP app captures the selected phone microphone, displays a live
level indicator, encodes audio as AAC beside H.264 video, and publishes both
over SRT. The relay authenticates and forwards that contribution feed. It does
not split, remix, normalize, or transcode it on the normal relay-to-OBS path.

The VISP OBS plugin adds the device as an FFmpeg Media Source with MPEG-TS
input, hardware decoding when available, low buffering, and automatic
reconnection. Audio from that source appears in the OBS Audio Mixer. The
[phone app documentation](https://docs.visp-stream.com/docs/phone-app) covers
camera and microphone selection, while the
[OBS plugin documentation](https://docs.visp-stream.com/docs/obs-remote-control)
explains one-click Media Source setup.

This means the first bad checkpoint usually tells you where to work. If the
phone meter is silent, an OBS filter cannot restore audio that was never
captured. If the OBS meter moves, changing the phone's SRT URL will not fix an
output-track mistake.

## 1. Prove the phone is capturing the intended microphone

Stop the contribution stream before changing the microphone. In the VISP app,
open settings and choose **System default** or the named input you actually
want. A wired microphone, USB interface, or Bluetooth headset may become the
system default when it connects, so do not assume the phone still uses its
built-in microphone.

Speak at the volume and distance you will use on air. The phone's microphone
indicator should respond clearly without remaining at its loudest state.
Then cover or disconnect the intended microphone briefly. If the meter keeps
responding normally, another input is active.

If the meter does not move:

1. confirm the operating system still grants microphone access to VISP;
2. disconnect and reconnect the external input;
3. select **System default**, then select the named input again;
4. stop and restart the camera session;
5. test once with the built-in microphone to separate an app problem from an
   accessory problem.

Use headphones for this test. A speaker beside the active phone microphone can
turn program audio or a call return into acoustic feedback.

## 2. Prove OBS receives exactly one copy

Start the phone feed and confirm that its device shows **Live** in the VISP
dashboard. Then select the scene containing the phone in OBS and speak while
watching the Audio Mixer.

The plugin should reuse or refresh the VISP Media Source for that device. If
you also created a manual Media Source with the same read URL, remove or mute
the duplicate. Two decoder sources reading the same microphone at slightly
different times sound like echo even though neither signal contains an
electronic echo.

For a clean isolation test:

1. mute every OBS audio source except the phone feed;
2. record one sentence and a hand clap;
3. listen to the recording with headphones;
4. unmute the remaining sources one at a time.

The source that reintroduces the second voice reveals the duplicate path.
Typical duplicates are the phone microphone plus a studio microphone hearing
the same room, two phones aimed at the same speaker, a second manual copy of
the VISP source, or a monitored signal being captured again as desktop audio.

Do not try to hide a duplicate with a noise gate. A gate may close between
words, but both copies still open when the person speaks.

## 3. Fix “the meter moves, but viewers hear nothing”

An active OBS meter proves that decoded audio reached the mixer. From there,
check the output path:

- the phone source is not muted in the Audio Mixer;
- its fader is not at minimum;
- **Audio Monitoring** is not set to **Monitor Only (mute output)** when the
  source should reach the broadcast;
- the source is assigned to the audio track selected for streaming;
- a nested scene or scene-specific source has not been muted elsewhere.

OBS's official
[Multiple Audio Track Recording Guide](https://obsproject.com/kb/multiple-audio-track-recording-guide)
shows where track assignments live in **Advanced Audio Properties**. It also
notes that ordinary playback software may expose only one recording track, so
test the same track your real output uses instead of assuming every enabled
track will be heard.

If the plugin-created source no longer connects, refresh it from **Tools →
VISP Remote Control**. Rotating the account read credential invalidates every
old VISP Media Source, so use the newly generated source rather than editing a
redacted URL by hand.

## 4. Remove echo by deleting the second route

Echo is usually routing, not a reason to add an echo-cancellation filter.
Draw one line for every way the voice enters OBS. Keep one intended line and
mute the others.

During normal streaming, leave the phone source on **Monitor Off** unless the
producer genuinely needs to hear it through the configured monitoring device.
Monitor Off does not mute the broadcast output. It only stops OBS from playing
that source through the monitoring device.

For a temporary sound check, route monitoring to headphones, not speakers.
OBS's current
[Audio Mixer Guide](https://obsproject.com/kb/audio-mixer-guide) recommends
checking the signal at the original device and again in OBS through Audio
Monitoring, then making and reviewing a normal recording before going live.

Also mute the public Twitch or Kick preview on every computer and phone in the
room. A delayed platform preview playing through speakers can be picked up by
the phone or studio microphone and return to the broadcast as a long echo.

## 5. Correct a fixed delay without breaking a good phone feed

First decide which signals should be synchronized.

The phone camera and phone microphone are encoded into the same contribution
feed. If their clap test is already aligned in a local OBS recording, leave
that source at zero offset. SRT latency adds delivery delay so lost packets
have time to be recovered; Haivision's
[SRT socket documentation](https://github.com/Haivision/srt/blob/master/docs/API/API-socket-options.md#srto_rcvlatency)
defines it as the receiver's minimum buffering delay. It is not an audio-only
delay control. OBS's
[SRT guide](https://obsproject.com/kb/srt-protocol-streaming-guide) documents
the URL latency value in microseconds, and the
[VISP encoder guide](https://docs.visp-stream.com/docs/broadcaster-setup)
explains how VISP recommends it from the measured route.

A fixed offset is useful when a **separate** audio route arrives earlier than
the phone video. Examples include venue sound sent through another device or a
studio microphone used during a conversation with the remote camera.

Record a visible and audible sync event that both relevant paths capture. In
OBS, open **Edit → Advanced Audio Properties** and add a positive **Sync
Offset** to the earlier audio source. Change one value at a time, record again,
and keep the smallest offset that aligns the event. Do not increase SRT
latency merely to move one mixer channel; that changes resilience and delay for
the whole contribution path.

If the phone feed starts synchronized but drifts later, a static offset is the
wrong first fix. Stop and restart the phone session and refresh the Media
Source, then repeat the recording. Check OBS for rendering or encoding
overload and test the real field network. A constant correction cannot repair
a delay that keeps changing.

## 6. Set a clean level and channel layout

Set gain in signal order: microphone or interface first, phone capture second,
and the OBS fader last. Once a microphone clips before encoding, lowering the
OBS fader only makes the distorted signal quieter.

OBS's Audio Mixer Guide explains its meter zones and peak indicator. Use them
as warning and comparison tools, then judge the result from a recording on
headphones and ordinary speakers. Do not chase one universal meter number for
speech, crowds, music, and ambient IRL sound.

If a third-party encoder presents a mono microphone in only the left or right
side of a stereo source, enable **Downmix to Mono** for that source in Advanced
Audio Properties. The native VISP apps already configure their microphone
contribution as a single voice-oriented channel, but Larix, Moblin, external
interfaces, and adapter cables can expose different channel layouts. Verify
both sides from the recording.

## Run a complete pre-stream audio rehearsal

Before the next Twitch or Kick broadcast:

1. choose the microphone while the phone stream is stopped;
2. confirm the phone meter responds to the intended input;
3. start publishing and confirm the device is **Live**;
4. confirm one phone source moves one OBS mixer meter;
5. mute all competing room microphones;
6. use headphones for temporary monitoring;
7. record speech, silence, a loud moment, and a clap;
8. correct only a measured fixed offset;
9. interrupt the phone connection and confirm audio returns after reconnect;
10. start a short private or low-risk platform test and check it from a second
    device with that device muted.

Keep the local recording. If the platform output sounds wrong while the local
file is clean, the contribution and OBS mix are already narrowed out; inspect
the final output configuration instead.

## Know what VISP does not fix

VISP gives the phone a revocable publishing path, relays its feed into OBS, and
keeps the Twitch or Kick destination in the home studio. On the standard
phone-to-OBS path, it does **not** transcode the feed, mix microphones, remove
room echo, normalize loudness, or bond cellular and Wi-Fi connections.

That boundary is useful during troubleshooting: capture problems belong at
the phone, transport problems belong on the contribution route, and final
mixing problems belong in OBS.
