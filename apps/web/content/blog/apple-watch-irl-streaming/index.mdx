To use Apple Watch during a Twitch or Kick IRL stream, **keep VISP open on the
paired iPhone, open the VISP Watch app, and swipe between the camera preview,
chat, stream health, and OBS scenes.** The watch shows the three most recent
chat messages, current stream state, microphone level, video format, Direct
viewer counts, and the scenes reported by a paired OBS instance.

This is a field monitor and a small scene switcher, not a second encoder or a
full OBS remote. The iPhone still captures and publishes the stream. The watch
does not hold provider credentials, reply to chat, moderate viewers, or start
and stop OBS.

![The iPhone sends camera media through the VISP relay to OBS while Twitch and Kick chat, stream status, camera previews, and OBS scene requests travel through the VISP app to the paired Apple Watch](./watch-signal-flow.svg)

## What the Apple Watch companion can do

| Watch page | What it shows or controls | Important limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Camera | A downscaled preview of the iPhone camera and its stream state | It is a low-frame-rate confidence view, not a production monitor |
| Chat | The latest three Twitch, Kick, or YouTube messages with sender and badges | Read-only; no reply, search, moderation, or history |
| Health | State, uptime, microphone tier, resolution, frame rate, reconnect detail, and Direct viewer counts | A dash means a provider did not return a current viewer count |
| OBS scenes | The current program scene and the scene list from the paired OBS plugin | Scene switching only; no mixer, source, recording, or stream-start controls |

The separation is deliberate. A walking creator can check whether the camera
is framed, notice a reconnect, read a recent message, or move OBS to **BRB**
without taking a hand off the rig for long. Detailed production work still
belongs on the iPhone or at OBS.

If you mainly need chat on the phone itself, start with
[reading Twitch and Kick chat on one phone](/blog/twitch-kick-chat-phone-obs).
If wrist control is unnecessary, the phone also offers the broader
[OBS remote-control surface](/blog/control-obs-phone-without-port-forwarding),
including stream start and stop.

## What you need

- An iPhone running the VISP native app and a paired Apple Watch running
  watchOS 10 or newer
- The VISP Watch companion installed on the watch
- Twitch or Kick chat linked and enabled in the iPhone app if you want messages
- A configured VISP Direct destination if you want its viewer count
- OBS Studio 31 with the VISP plugin paired if you want scene switching
- Short, distinct OBS scene names and a rehearsed fallback scene

The Apple Watch path is iPhone-only. An Android phone can publish through VISP,
but it cannot act as the companion for this watchOS app. See the current
[phone-app requirements](https://docs.visp-stream.com/docs/phone-app) before
building the rest of the workflow.

## 1. Prepare the iPhone stream first

Complete the normal iPhone setup before involving the watch. Sign in, select
the camera, resolution, frame rate, microphone, and destination, then run a
short private test. The watch mirrors useful state from that running app; it
does not configure the camera or destination for you.

For a home-studio production, follow
[the phone-to-OBS guide](/blog/use-phone-as-remote-camera-obs). The iPhone sends
one H.264/AAC contribution feed to VISP, the relay forwards it without
transcoding, and OBS owns the Twitch or Kick program. For a no-OBS workflow,
[VISP Direct](/blog/what-is-visp-direct) performs a separate destination encode
at the relay.

These two paths explain one detail on the Health page: viewer counts describe
the selected Direct outputs. They are not an independent measurement of every
OBS destination.

## 2. Enable Twitch or Kick chat on the iPhone

Open **Settings → Advanced → Connections** in the VISP app. Link each provider
you want to follow and enable chat separately. Send one test message from
another account and wait for the provider to show connected before relying on
the watch.

Twitch delivers the read path through its
[`channel.chat.message` EventSub subscription](https://dev.twitch.tv/docs/eventsub/eventsub-subscription-types/#channelchatmessage),
which requires chat-read authorization. Kick documents the equivalent
[`chat.message.sent` event](https://github.com/KickEngineering/KickDevDocs/blob/main/events/event-types.md).
VISP forwards accepted messages to the signed-in iPhone, and the iPhone includes
the latest three in the watch snapshot.

The watch view is intentionally compact. It joins the message fragments into
plain readable text, shows provider and user badges, and limits each message to
two lines. Use Twitch or Kick's own moderation tools when chat is busy; the
watch cannot delete, time out, ban, reply, or recover older messages.

## 3. Pair OBS and simplify the scene list

To switch scenes from the watch, install the VISP plugin in OBS Studio 31 and
pair it with the same VISP account. The full procedure is in
[OBS remote control](https://docs.visp-stream.com/docs/obs-remote-control).
The plugin reports its current program scene and scene names through an
outbound authenticated connection, so OBS needs no inbound router port.

OBS also includes a much broader WebSocket control system. Its official
[Remote Control Guide](https://obsproject.com/kb/remote-control-guide) describes
that interface and recommends password protection. Use it when you need mixer,
source, filter, recording, or automation control. The VISP watch companion is
the narrower field option: it only chooses the program scene exposed by the
paired VISP plugin.

Rename operational scenes before the rehearsal. **Field**, **Wide**, **BRB**,
and **End** are safer on a small display than several names that differ only at
the end. The watch marks the current scene, disables another request while a
switch is pending, and reports an error if OBS is offline or busy.

## 4. Open VISP on both devices

Start VISP on the iPhone, then open the companion on Apple Watch. Swipe through
its four pages:

1. **Camera** confirms the current composition with downscaled preview frames.
2. **Chat** shows connected providers and the newest three messages.
3. **Health** shows live state, elapsed time, microphone activity, format,
   viewer counts, and reconnect information.
4. **OBS scenes** marks the current program scene and lets you request another.

Keep the iPhone app running in the foreground for live watch data. VISP sends
small status snapshots through Apple's Watch Connectivity framework and sends
preview frames only while the watch app is reachable. Apple defines
[`isReachable`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchconnectivity/wcsession/isreachable)
as live-message availability between the counterpart apps; it is not a promise
that two closed apps will continue an interactive control session.

The companion keeps no separate Twitch, Kick, or OBS credential. Apple
describes Watch Connectivity as the two-way data channel between an iOS app and
its paired watchOS app. In this design, the watch receives compact snapshots
from the iPhone and sends a scene request back through that same paired-device
channel.

## 5. Rehearse the wrist workflow

Run one end-to-end rehearsal before a public stream:

1. Start a private or otherwise safe test from the iPhone.
2. Confirm that the Camera page updates and matches the intended framing.
3. Speak normally and check that the Health page microphone tier moves.
4. Send one Twitch and one Kick test message, if both are enabled.
5. Switch from **Field** to a harmless test scene and confirm the program in
   OBS.
6. Disconnect the iPhone briefly from the network and confirm that reconnect
   information appears while OBS holds the fallback scene.
7. Close VISP on the iPhone and learn the watch's **Open VISP on iPhone** state.

Do not treat the tiny Camera page as proof that viewers receive healthy video.
It verifies the local capture path. VISP's live status describes the phone
publisher, while OBS and the platform dashboard verify later parts of the
chain. The guide to
[diagnosing dropped frames across an IRL stream](/blog/obs-dropped-frames-irl-streaming)
explains those separate checkpoints.

## Use one simple live routine

During the stream, make the watch a glanceable exception monitor rather than a
second dashboard:

- Check **Health** when the phone vibrates, the picture seems delayed, or chat
  reports trouble.
- Check **Chat** when you need the latest context without covering the iPhone
  camera preview.
- Use **OBS scenes** for a rehearsed move to **BRB**, **Wide**, or **Field**.
- Return to the iPhone or contact the home producer for anything more complex.

A scene command needs a reachable watch-to-iPhone session, the running VISP
app, the VISP service, a connected OBS plugin, and the OBS computer. If any link
is down, stop retrying and use the fallback plan. VISP cannot turn a lost
control path into an emergency guarantee.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can Apple Watch stream to Twitch or Kick by itself?

No. The watch companion neither captures nor publishes the program. The iPhone
is the encoder; VISP relays its contribution to OBS or encodes configured Direct
destinations.

### Can I start or stop OBS from the watch?

Not currently. The watch can switch the current program scene. Use the VISP
iPhone control or the producer at OBS to start and stop the broadcast.

### Why does the watch show only three chat messages?

The companion is designed for quick field glances, so the iPhone sends only the
latest three messages. Use the phone or provider dashboard for history and
moderation.

### Why is the viewer count a dash?

The selected Direct provider did not return a current count. A dash is an
unknown value, not zero, and an OBS-only destination is outside this Direct
count path.

### Does the watch keep working when VISP is closed on the iPhone?

No interactive workflow should depend on that. The watch may have a last
application snapshot, but live preview and scene commands require the paired
iPhone app to be reachable. VISP shows **Open VISP on iPhone** instead of
presenting stale data as live.
