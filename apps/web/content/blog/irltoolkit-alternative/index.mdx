[IRLToolkit](https://irltoolkit.com/) charges **$129 per month** for its Standard
plan and **$179** for Advanced. That buys a managed cloud OBS instance, SRTLA
ingest, drop protection, and a dashboard your moderators can drive while you are
outside. It is a real product solving a real problem, and for a full-time IRL
creator the price is often justified.

It is also the wrong purchase for a lot of people who make it. **Choose a cloud
OBS service when you need scenes, overlays, and moderator control while you are
away from a computer. Choose something cheaper when what you actually need is
"get my phone camera to Twitch reliably."**

This post is about telling those two situations apart.

Read the bonding row before the price row. If you walk through areas with patchy
coverage and need two cellular modems aggregated into one usable pipe, the paid
services and BELABOX do something VISP does not do, and no amount of price
difference changes that. Skip to [when to pay](#when-paying-is-the-right-call).

## What the $129 actually buys

Three things, and they are worth separating because most people only need one.

**A computer you do not own.** Cloud OBS means an OBS instance runs on someone
else's machine, so your home PC can be off. This is the part most buyers are
really after, and it is the part that no longer requires a subscription.

**Scenes, overlays, and alerts.** A full OBS in the cloud gives you starting
screens, chat overlays, sponsor bugs, and scene switching driven from a phone or
by a moderator. If your show has production value beyond a camera, this is the
reason to pay.

**SRTLA bonding and drop protection.** Multiple network links aggregated, plus a
holding screen when the feed dies. For a creator whose stream is their income and
whose route is unpredictable, this is insurance, not luxury.

## What VISP does instead

VISP moves the encode into the relay rather than into a cloud OBS. Your phone or
browser sends one feed; the relay distribution-encodes it and delivers straight
to Twitch, Kick, or YouTube. The computer at home stays off, and there is no OBS
in the path unless you want one.

That covers the first item on the list above, and covers it for free during the
beta. It does not give you cloud scenes, and it does not aggregate bandwidth.

Two things it does that the paid services do not:

- **The phone app is part of the product.** iOS, Android, and a browser publisher,
  rather than "configure Larix or Moblin yourself." Details in the
  [phone app docs](https://docs.visp-stream.com/docs/phone-app).
- **Chat is told when the signal drops.** The relay knows the difference
  between a dropout it is holding on a BRB card and a stream that ended, and
  posts the right one to Twitch, Kick, and YouTube chat. Details in
  [IRL chat bot alerts](/blog/irl-chat-bot-alerts-without-a-pc).
- **Your stream key never reaches the device.** VISP resolves authorized platform
  credentials on the server while starting the output and never returns them to
  the publisher. A lost or borrowed phone does not leak your key. Compare that to
  pasting a key into a third-party encoder, as covered in
  [adding a remote guest to OBS without a stream key](/blog/remote-guest-obs-without-stream-key).

If you later want scenes, the same contribution feed can be read in your own OBS
without disturbing the platform encode. That is the
[phone as a remote camera](/blog/use-phone-as-remote-camera-obs) workflow, and it
is additive rather than a different product.

## When paying is the right call

Be honest about which of these describe you:

- **You need real bonding today.** Two or more cellular modems aggregated, not
  duplicated. BELABOX or an SRTLA service is the answer. VISP's native app can
  duplicate packets across Wi-Fi and cellular, which survives one link dropping,
  but two half-speed connections do not add up to one fast one.
- **A moderator runs your production while you walk.** Cloud OBS with dashboard
  access is built for exactly this.
- **Your show is scene-heavy and you are never near a computer.** Cloud OBS.
- **Downtime costs you money.** Paid drop protection and a support contract are
  worth $129 to a full-time creator, and the calculus is different from a hobby.

## When free is enough

- The phone is the whole show — a walk, a ride, an event, a talking-head segment.
- You want to *try* IRL streaming before committing to a subscription.
- You already have OBS at home and only need the camera to arrive reliably.
- You want to stream to Twitch, Kick, and YouTube without a machine running at
  home, which is the [no-PC workflow](/blog/stream-to-twitch-without-a-pc).

## Switching, or just testing

Nothing here requires a decision in advance. Authorize a platform once, publish
from the phone app or the browser, and see whether the missing pieces actually
matter to your show. If they do, the paid services are still there, and you will
know precisely which feature you are buying instead of guessing.

If it turns out you need bonding, we would rather you spent the money on BELABOX
than on us — that is a real capability we do not have yet, and
[our comparison of BELABOX and LiveU Solo](/blog/visp-vs-belabox-vs-liveu-solo)
covers where each one fits.

## Test the difference before subscribing

Use the same route, phone, resolution, and target bitrate for a fair comparison.
First run the simplest path directly through VISP. Record where the connection
becomes unstable, whether the phone overheats, and whether viewers actually miss
scenes or overlays. Then trial a bonded or cloud-OBS service on the same route.
Changing the route and encoder at the same time makes the result meaningless.

Watch the finished platform broadcast, not only the preview on the phone. Check
speech intelligibility, motion detail, time spent buffering, and recovery after
airplane mode or a deliberate Wi-Fi disconnect. Ask a moderator to note the
times of visible failures so they can be matched to relay and encoder health.

The test may show that cloud OBS solves a production problem but not the weak
radio segment, or that bonding fixes the route while scenes add no value. Those
are different purchases. A service combining both can still be convenient, but
you should know which capability earns the monthly fee.

## Account for the whole workflow

The field connection is only one part of reliability. A home OBS workflow also
depends on home power, home upload, operating-system updates, and remote access.
A managed cloud OBS removes those household dependencies and can be worth paying
for even on an excellent mobile connection. Direct relay output removes the OBS
machine entirely, which is simpler still when the camera feed is the finished
program.

Think about who operates the stream when something breaks. A solo creator needs
controls that are safe to use while moving. A team may benefit from a moderator
dashboard and a cloud scene switcher. Do not pay for collaborative controls that
no one will operate, and do not expect a solo workflow to cover a staffed show.

There is also an exit-cost question. Keep copies of scene assets, document
encoder settings, and avoid making the only version of a show depend on a
proprietary dashboard. VISP is GPL-2.0 and self-hostable; a hosted alternative
may offer more convenience and support. Neither property automatically wins,
but it changes what happens if pricing, limits, or your production needs change.

The practical answer is therefore deliberately narrow: use VISP while transport
and direct delivery are enough. Pay IRLToolkit, Streamable.run, or another
specialist when measured network conditions require bonding or the show actively
uses a remotely operated studio. That boundary keeps the free setup honest and
makes the subscription easy to justify when you cross it.
