The safest IRL stream test happens in layers. First, send the phone through
VISP into OBS without any platform output enabled. Record the complete program
in OBS and watch it back. Then test the final destination separately. Twitch
offers a hidden bandwidth test through Twitch Inspector. Kick's official OBS
workflow goes live when you click **Start Streaming**, so treat any Kick ingest
check as a short public verification.

Do not start with a public test. An off-air recording catches most expensive
mistakes: a silent microphone, the wrong camera, unreadable overlays, a failed
fallback scene, an unstable field route, or private information in the frame.

![A phone feed passes through a relay into an OBS recording while the final audience connection remains inactive](./irl-stream-test-path.svg)

## The short answer

Use this order:

1. Turn off Direct outputs for the VISP device that you will test.
2. Start the phone feed and confirm that the VISP dashboard shows the device as
   live.
3. Check the Media Source, audio meters, scenes, and fallback in OBS.
4. Record a realistic 10 to 15 minute route without starting the OBS stream.
5. Watch the recording with headphones and inspect **View → Stats** in OBS.
6. Run a Twitch Inspector bandwidth test, or plan a brief Kick live check.
7. Restore the final platform settings only after the off-air test passes.

This sequence separates three different questions. Can the phone reach OBS?
Can OBS produce a clean program? Can the platform receive and play that
program? A single green preview cannot answer all three.

## Keep the first test off-air

VISP has two output paths, and the distinction matters during a rehearsal.

In the normal phone-to-OBS path, the phone encodes H.264 video and AAC audio.
VISP relays that contribution to an OBS Media Source without transcoding it.
OBS owns the scenes, recording, final encode, and Twitch or Kick destination.
You can therefore run the phone and record OBS without sending anything to a
platform.

With [VISP Direct](https://docs.visp-stream.com/docs/direct-output), the relay
starts a separate distribution encode for every enabled destination when the
publishing device connects. The **Go Live** button can then make Twitch or Kick
public without OBS. Before an off-air test, stop the device and open **Settings
→ Stream to**. Turn off Twitch, Kick, YouTube, and any custom Direct output for
that device.

The switches are locked while the device is publishing. This prevents a live
output from moving under an active feed. The current
[native Direct settings](https://github.com/PohinaGroup/visp/blob/main/apps/native/src/components/stream-settings-direct-section.tsx)
and [Direct ownership logic](https://github.com/PohinaGroup/visp/blob/main/packages/api/src/direct.ts)
enforce that boundary.

If **Never drop again** is holding an existing Direct broadcast on a BRB card,
end that broadcast before changing test settings. Turning off Direct on an idle
device is the cleanest off-air state.

## Test the phone-to-OBS path

Set up the same rig that you plan to carry. Use the same phone, camera, mount,
microphone, power bank, case, mobile connections, resolution, frame rate, and
bitrate. A desk test on home Wi-Fi proves a different setup.

1. Open OBS and select the scene collection that you will use live.
2. Start the VISP phone app, but leave OBS streaming stopped.
3. Confirm that the device row on the VISP dashboard shows **Live**.
4. Wait for the phone Media Source to show moving video and audio in OBS.
5. Start an OBS recording.
6. Walk the weakest section of the real route for at least 10 minutes.

The [VISP phone app guide](https://docs.visp-stream.com/docs/phone-app) explains
the automatic device binding. The [encoder and fallback
guide](https://docs.visp-stream.com/docs/broadcaster-setup) covers the imported
OBS Media Source, SRT latency, reconnect behavior, and fallback scene.

Do not press the phone's **Start OBS stream** control during this stage. That
button asks the paired OBS plugin to start the configured platform output. The
phone contribution and the OBS output are independent, which is exactly what
makes an off-air test possible.

## Make the rehearsal behave like the real show

Standing still in a quiet room hides the failures that matter outdoors. Run the
actions that your stream actually needs:

- walk past the known weak mobile area;
- turn, pan, and change exposure between bright and dark locations;
- speak at normal distance while traffic or a crowd is audible;
- switch every OBS scene that the field creator or producer will use;
- trigger one alert and check that it does not cover a face or caption;
- disconnect the phone long enough for the fallback scene to appear;
- reconnect and confirm that OBS returns to the live scene without restarting
  the public output.

Record the failure test too. The OBS preview may look acceptable while the
recording reveals clipped speech, one-sided audio, a delayed microphone, or a
transition that exposes an empty source.

OBS's official [Quick Start Guide](https://obsproject.com/kb/quick-start-guide)
recommends a multi-minute test before the first stream or recording. Its
[Audio Mixer Guide](https://obsproject.com/kb/audio-mixer-guide) specifically
advises recording a normal session and listening back before going live.

## Review the recording, not only the preview

Watch the complete file at normal speed with headphones. Do not scrub only the
first and last minute.

Check these details:

- speech is clear and reaches both left and right channels;
- the camera stays correctly framed while walking and turning;
- captions, chat, and alerts remain readable against bright and dark scenes;
- the fallback appears after a lost feed and disappears after recovery;
- no address, license plate, notification, reflection, or bystander remains in
  frame longer than intended;
- the picture does not freeze while audio continues;
- the phone reaches normal operating temperature without the frame rate
  collapsing.

Open **View → Stats** before the test. Recording exercises scene rendering and
encoding, so rising rendering or encoding lag still matters even when the
platform output is off. Network dropped frames in OBS apply to its outgoing
stream, so that counter becomes relevant during the platform stage. The
[OBS encoding guide](https://obsproject.com/kb/encoding-performance-troubleshooting)
separates computer load from connection trouble.

For phone and relay health, watch VISP's live bitrate, round-trip time, loss,
and reconnect attempts. If the feed fails before it reaches OBS, use the
[IRL dropped-frames guide](/blog/obs-dropped-frames-irl-streaming) to isolate
the phone-to-relay and relay-to-OBS checkpoints.

## Run a hidden Twitch bandwidth test

Twitch supports a test stream that does not appear live on your channel. Its
[Stream Key FAQ](https://help.twitch.tv/s/article/twitch-stream-key-faq?language=en_US)
instructs encoders to append `?bandwidthtest=true` to the stream key. Start the
OBS stream with that test key, then open
[Twitch Inspector](https://inspector.twitch.tv/) to review stream health.

Keep the normal stream key secret. Do not paste it into chat, screenshots, log
notes, or a shared checklist. Follow Twitch's current dashboard instructions
when you need to reveal it.

Run the test long enough to include the route and home-network conditions that
matter. Twitch Inspector adds the OBS-to-Twitch ingest leg to the rehearsal,
but it does not replace watching your OBS recording. Inspector can show a
stable ingest while the scene still contains bad audio or private visual
details.

After the test, restore the normal Twitch connection. Verify that the
`?bandwidthtest=true` suffix is gone before the scheduled broadcast.

## Treat a Kick ingest check as live

Kick's official [OBS setup guide](https://help.kick.com/en/articles/7066931-how-to-stream-on-kick-com)
defines **Start Streaming** in OBS as the **Go Live** step. The guide does not
put a private rehearsal between OBS and the public channel.

For that reason, keep the first Kick test inside VISP and OBS. Record the
program off-air, fix every local problem, and check the Kick title, category,
server URL, stream key, H.264 output, CBR, and two-second keyframe interval
before the final stage.

When you must prove the Kick ingest and player, plan a short live verification.
Tell moderators what it is, avoid promoting it, and stop as soon as the public
player, audio, and recovery behavior are confirmed. Kick says that stopping
OBS ends the stream and saves a VOD. Review that test VOD according to your
channel workflow.

Do not describe a Kick test as private unless Kick publishes and you use a
specific private-testing feature. A quiet live check is still live.

## Test VISP Direct separately

An OBS recording shows the original contribution feed. It does not show the
separate encode that VISP Direct sends to viewers. If you plan to stream with
Direct, complete the off-air phone-to-OBS recording first, then re-enable the
intended Direct destination and run a short platform check.

Watch the actual Twitch or Kick player from a muted second device on a
different connection. Confirm the public resolution, audio, title, category,
viewer-facing overlays, and recovery behavior. Muting prevents the player's
audio from feeding back into the phone microphone.

Direct destinations fail independently, and each uses its own relay slot. A
successful Twitch output does not prove that Kick also started. If you
multistream, check each destination rather than trusting one status indicator.

## Use a pass or fail sheet

Keep the checklist short enough to use before every event.

| Checkpoint | Pass condition |
| --- | --- |
| Phone → VISP | Device stays live on the real route with acceptable bitrate, RTT, loss, and heat |
| VISP → OBS | Video and audio recover after a forced disconnect without recreating the source |
| OBS program | Full recording has clean audio, correct scenes, readable overlays, and no private details |
| OBS performance | Rendering and encoding lag stay at zero or within an accepted tested limit |
| Twitch | Inspector reports a stable hidden bandwidth test with the final output settings |
| Kick | A planned short live check reaches the public player with correct metadata and audio |
| Direct | Every enabled destination starts, plays, and recovers independently |

If one row fails, fix that checkpoint and repeat only the affected stage. Do
not change bitrate, frame rate, SRT latency, audio filters, and scenes at the
same time. One change produces a result you can understand.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I test a Twitch stream without going live?

Yes. Twitch's official method appends `?bandwidthtest=true` to the stream key
and reports the result in Twitch Inspector. The test checks ingest health
without putting the channel live.

### Can I test a Kick stream privately?

Kick's current OBS setup guide treats **Start Streaming** as **Go Live**. Keep
your first rehearsal off-air in OBS, and treat any final Kick ingest check as a
short public broadcast unless Kick documents another mode.

### Does starting the VISP phone app make me public?

It depends on the device's outputs. With no Direct destination enabled, the
phone publishes to the relay and OBS can read it without going live on Twitch
or Kick. With Direct enabled, publishing starts the selected platform outputs.

### Does an OBS recording prove what Direct viewers see?

No. OBS reads the contribution feed, while Direct makes a separate platform
encode. Watch the actual platform player during the final Direct check.

### How long should an IRL test run?

Use at least 10 to 15 minutes for a basic check. For an important event, cover
the real route, weak network area, normal phone temperature, scene changes,
audio conditions, and one forced reconnect.
