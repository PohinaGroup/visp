# VISP short-form scripts — "Before you buy" series

Six shootable scripts in the price-card format from `MARKETING.md` §4. Every one
follows the same five beats: **price card → credit the incumbent → the swap →
the limit → CTA.** The limit beat is not optional. Publishing the case where
VISP loses is what makes the other five believable.

Post as a numbered series. Same end card, same voice, same 5 beats — the format
becomes recognizable after three of them.

---

## Shared production spec

**Format.** 1080×1920, 30fps. Shoot the field and OBS material in 4K so
punch-ins are real crops, not upscales. Screen-record OBS at 60fps or the scene
cuts read as stutter.

**Safe area.** Keep the bottom 420px and right 200px clear of anything that
matters — TikTok's UI sits there. Hook text goes upper third.

**Type.** Barlow SemiBold for the spoken line, IBM Plex Mono for every number,
price, and HUD callout. No neon, no gradients. Color only for signal state:
green live, amber degraded, red lost. Everything else is mono.

**Every price gets a source stamp.** Small mono line under the number:
`Elgato HD60 X · elgato.com · Aug 2026`. Without it the top comment is "that's
not the price" and the whole series loses its footing.

**Audio.** Original audio, close-miked, dry. Room tone bed under field shots at
about −24 LUFS; nothing under the price cards — silence makes the number land.
No trending sound. This format's credibility comes from sounding like a person
who owns the gear, not from an edit that sounds like an ad.

**VO delivery.** Flat, unhurried, slightly bored. Never sell. The credit beat is
delivered sincerely or the whole thing curdles. Budget ~2.2 words/second — the
scripts below are written to that, leave the air in.

**Captions.** Burned, not auto-generated. The limit line is spoken *and* burned;
never only burned.

**Export.** Clean master, uploaded natively per platform. No platform watermark
on anything you cross-post.

**Shoot once.** Scripts 1, 2, 10 and 11 all need: two phones, a cheap tripod, the
home OBS rig, a walk outside. Block one afternoon, get all four, plus B-roll for
the tutorials.

## 1. Before you buy a capture card

**Length:** 24s · **Props:** capture card (or its listing on screen), coiled
HDMI, a camera, an old phone, a €15 tripod, OBS on the monitor.

| Time | Shot | On-screen | VO |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:03 | Overhead. Hands drop the card and a coiled HDMI onto a hard table. Audible thunk. | `Before you buy a capture card` | "Before you buy a capture card." |
| 0:03–0:08 | Punch-in: the cable running across the floor to a camera on sticks. | `€180 card` / `+ camera` / `+ 5m of cable` (mono, stamping in one per line) | "One-eighty. And it still needs a camera, and a cable across the room." |
| 0:08–0:11 | Beat on the camera. No VO. Just the room. | — | *(silence)* |
| 0:11–0:17 | One unbroken shot: old phone clipped to the cheap tripod, thumb taps Go Live, whip-pan to the monitor as the source appears. | `free` | "This is a phone from a drawer, on a fifteen-euro tripod. Second angle, no cable." |
| 0:17–0:21 | OBS cutting between the two angles, side by side in the multiview. | `uncompressed · instant` vs `compressed · ~1s behind` | "The card looks better. It's uncompressed, and it's instant." |
| 0:21–0:24 | End card. MeterMark. | `VISP · free during beta · open source` | "This one's free, and it works tonight." |

**Edit notes.** Cut on the thunk at 0:03. The 0:08 silence is the whole ad —
don't fill it. The whip-pan at 0:11 must be one take with no hidden cut; if the
source takes eight seconds to appear, show the eight seconds. Hold the split
multiview long enough to actually compare, ~2s minimum.

**Caption.** Capture card gives you the better picture. A phone in the drawer
gives you the second angle tonight. Both are fine answers.
`#OBSStudio #IRLStreaming`

**Pinned comment.** "Yes, the card is better — uncompressed and no network in
the path. This is about the angle you don't have yet, not the one you do."

## 2. Before you buy a switcher

**Length:** 26s · **Props:** ATEM Mini (or listing), the OBS scene collection,
one phone in a pocket.

| Time | Shot | On-screen | VO |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:03 | Top-down, hand slides the switcher into frame. | `Before you buy a switcher` + `€309` (mono, with source stamp) | "Before you buy a switcher." |
| 0:03–0:09 | Macro on fingers hitting the physical buttons — real button clicks in the mix. Cut to program monitor changing. | — | "Hardware switchers are instant. They never crash, and they don't care what your PC is doing." |
| 0:09–0:13 | Cut to the OBS scene collection: four scenes, named, already built. | `you already built these` | "But your show already lives in scenes." |
| 0:13–0:21 | One take: hand pulls the phone from a pocket, taps a scene in OBS Remote, whip to the monitor, OBS cuts. | — | "So you already own a switcher. This is it, from three hundred metres away." |
| 0:21–0:24 | Back on the hardware. | `software · over a network · slower` | "It's software, over a network. It will be slower than the buttons." |
| 0:24–0:26 | End card. | `VISP · free during beta · open source` | "Try the one you own first." |

**Edit notes.** Sound-design the button clicks up — they're the reason people
want a switcher, so let them be seductive. Then the phone tap at 0:13 gets the
same treatment: same click, quieter. Match-cut those two moments and the ad
makes its argument without the VO.

**Caption.** Hardware switchers are instant and bulletproof. If your show is
already OBS scenes, you may already own the switcher.
`#OBSStudio #Streaming`

**Pinned comment.** "An ATEM is genuinely better if you switch live cameras in
one room. This is for when the camera isn't in the room."

## 3. Before you subscribe to cloud OBS

**Length:** 26s · **Props:** a pricing page, the PC under your desk, one phone.

| Time | Shot | On-screen | VO |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:04 | Screen record: cursor rests on the monthly price. Mono type stamps `× 12` and the annual number underneath. | `$__/mo` → `$__/yr` (source-stamped) | "Before you subscribe to cloud OBS." |
| 0:04–0:10 | Slow push on the pricing page. | — | "It's real value. It renders your whole show when your computer is off." |
| 0:10–0:15 | Cut to under the desk: tower running, fans turning, power LED. Handheld, unglamorous. | `mine isn't off` | "Mine isn't off. It's running OBS right now, and it will be all night." |
| 0:15–0:21 | Phone outside → the same tower's OBS picks it up. Split screen. | — | "So the phone goes to my machine, not to somebody's cloud." |
| 0:21–0:24 | Back to the pricing page, cursor idle. | `laptop in a hotel → pay for the cloud` | "If you stream off a laptop in a hotel, pay for the cloud. It's the right tool." |
| 0:24–0:26 | End card. | `VISP · free during beta · open source` | "If your tower's already on, you're paying twice." |

**Edit notes.** Shoot the under-desk shot badly on purpose — dust, cables,
handheld. The contrast with the polished pricing page is the argument. Do not
name or logo the competitor's page beyond what's legible in passing; blur the
brand if legal is nervous, the pricing shape reads fine on its own.

**Caption.** Cloud OBS means your PC can be off. If your PC is already on, that
subscription is buying you something you have.
`#OBSStudio #LiveStreaming`

**Pinned comment.** "Cloud OBS wins when you don't have a machine at home, or
you want it rendering while you sleep. Different problem."

## 6. Before you buy a second data plan

**Length:** 27s · **Props:** two SIM cards, the link-stats HUD, a walk with one
predictable dead spot.

> **Claim discipline.** Say "two links, one feed." Never say bonded internet,
> never say unbreakable, never imply the camera feed can't drop. The data line
> is mandatory and must be spoken, not just burned.

| Time | Shot | On-screen | VO |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:03 | Two SIM cards dropped on a dark table, macro. | `Before you buy a second data plan` + `€__/mo × 2` | "Before you buy a second data plan." |
| 0:03–0:08 | The two SIMs, one finger sliding the second one forward. | `12 months` | "Two lines is the standard fix for a bad connection. It's also two bills, for a year." |
| 0:08–0:16 | Macro on the link-stats HUD, held long: Wi-Fi green, cellular green, both carrying. Walk into the dead spot — one bar goes amber — picture holds. | `Wi-Fi` `Cellular` (signal-state colors only) | "In broadcast mode both links carry the same feed. One dips, the picture holds." |
| 0:16–0:22 | Same HUD, wider, phone in hand while walking. | `sends the video twice` `≈ 2× mobile data` | "It sends the video twice. So it's roughly double the mobile data, the whole time it's on." |
| 0:22–0:25 | Settings toggle flicking on and off. | — | "Test it on the SIM you already have." |
| 0:25–0:27 | End card. | `VISP · free during beta · open source` | "Then decide if it's worth a second one." |

**Edit notes.** The HUD hold at 0:08 is the money shot — 8 seconds, one take, no
cuts, so nobody can accuse the recovery of being an edit. Let the amber bar sit
there. Shoot the dead spot three or four times to get a clean one; use the take
where the picture visibly wobbles and comes back, not the flawless one.

**Burned lower-third for the full HUD sequence:** `Two links, one feed. VISP
does not bond networks and does not guarantee an uninterrupted camera feed.`

**Caption.** Two links, one feed — and roughly double the mobile data while it's
running. Try it on the SIM you have before you sign for another.
`#IRLStreaming #Streaming`

**Pinned comment.** "This isn't SRTLA bonding. Both links carry the same feed,
which is why it costs double the data. Honest tradeoff, not magic."

## 10. Before you trade in your old phone

**Length:** 26s · **Props:** a real trade-in quote on screen, the drawer, the
old phone, a charger, a tripod.

| Time | Shot | On-screen | VO |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:03 | Screen record: trade-in quote, the number highlighted. | `Before you trade in your old phone` + `€__` (source-stamped, real quote) | "Before you trade in your old phone." |
| 0:03–0:08 | The number, held. Nothing else. | `≈ 2 months of a streaming subscription` | "That's about two months of a streaming subscription. Once." |
| 0:08–0:16 | Drawer opens. Old phone, dead. Plug it in, boot it, clip it to the tripod. Real time, real boot screen. | — | "Or it's a camera. Today." |
| 0:16–0:21 | Two angles cutting in OBS: main camera, then the drawer phone. | `angle 2` | "It needs power and something to sit on. It doesn't need to be your phone anymore." |
| 0:21–0:24 | Back on the trade-in page, cursor hovering the button, not clicking. | — | "The trade-in is worth what it's worth." |
| 0:24–0:26 | End card. | `VISP · free during beta · open source` | "Check the drawer first." |

**Edit notes.** Do not clean the drawer. Do not skip the boot screen — the fact
that it's a genuinely old, slow, forgotten phone is the entire proof. Use the
oldest device that actually works; if it's a 2019 handset, say the model out
loud in the caption.

**Caption.** The trade-in value of a three-year-old phone is about two months of
a subscription. Or it's your second angle, permanently.
`#IRLStreaming #OBSStudio`

**Pinned comment.** "Tested on a <model, year>. It needs to stay plugged in —
publishing eats battery."

## 11. Before you buy the backpack (the one where we lose)

**Length:** 30s · **Props:** a bonded backpack rig listing, a walk to a park, the
home OBS with alerts live.

> This is the anchor of the series. It is not a competitor takedown — it is the
> ad that names the case where you should spend the €1,800. Post it third or
> fourth, once the format is recognizable. Never say "replaces a backpack."

| Time | Shot | On-screen | VO |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:04 | Backpack rig listing, price large. Held, no motion. | `€1,800` (source-stamped) | "I'm not going to tell you to cancel the backpack." |
| 0:04–0:12 | Stock-feel B-roll or a still: a rig on someone's back, moving. If you don't have the footage, hold the listing photo — do not fake it. | `moving vehicle` `4 hours` `bad coverage` | "If you stream four hours from a moving car, in a bad coverage area, buy the backpack. It's built for exactly that. This isn't." |
| 0:12–0:20 | Cut hard to: front door, walk down a street to a park. Handheld phone, daylight, ordinary. | — | "This is a walk to the park on a Saturday." |
| 0:20–0:25 | The home OBS: the walk is in the scene, a sub alert fires over it, chat scrolling on the phone. | `same OBS · same alerts` | "Same OBS. Same alerts. Same overlay. A phone you already own." |
| 0:25–0:28 | Back outside, walking away from camera. | — | "That's a smaller problem than the backpack solves." |
| 0:28–0:30 | End card. | `VISP · free during beta · open source` | "Buy the backpack when the park stops being enough." |

**Edit notes.** The 0:12 cut should feel like a change of weather — loud
listing page and hard mono type, then daylight and room tone. Let the walk
breathe for a full 8 seconds with no text on screen at all; it's the only
moment in the series that isn't arguing. The alert at 0:20 must be a real one
from a real stream, timestamp visible if possible.

**Caption.** Backpacks are better at the hard job. This is for the easy one.
`#IRLStreaming #TwitchStreamer`

**Pinned comment.** "Genuinely — if you're doing long-form mobile IRL in bad
coverage, buy the proper rig. This is for people who want to go outside
occasionally without rebuilding their show."
